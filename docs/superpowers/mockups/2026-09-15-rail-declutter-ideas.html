Five ways to keep the rail clean without losing anything

Each idea switches the mock rail on the left into that mode. Ideas 1, 2 and 5 are one family: hide, sleep and tidy all put rows on a shelf you can see and reopen, so nothing silently disappears. Ideas 3 and 4 change what a row costs on screen.

Hiding a chat is only safe if three things stay true, and the mock shows each one:

  • The count never lies. The group header reads 5 · 2 hidden even while the group is collapsed, so a folded client still tells you something is tucked away.
  • The shelf is a row, not a setting. A dashed one-line shelf at the foot of the group says 2 hidden · Kittle Stuff, Listing Ad. Hover lists the names; click opens them in place with an Unhide beside each.
  • Activity overrides hiding. If a hidden chat rings the bell, finishes a task or exits, it resurfaces on its own with a small WOKE tag until you click it. You cannot forget a chat that is doing something.

Global escape hatch: Ctrl+Shift+H or the footer link shows every hidden chat across all groups; search always finds them.

Most clutter is not chats you want hidden, it is chats that finished. Instead of you hiding them one by one, an exited or idle chat folds into the shelf after a quiet period you choose. Same shelf, same count, same wake-on-activity rule as idea 1, so the two share one mechanism: hide is "sleep now", sleep is "hide later".

  • The shelf reads asleep · 3 and lists names on hover.
  • A sleeping chat keeps its folder and profile; reopening resumes where it was (the resume path that already exists).
  • Per-group override: a client you are actively on can be set to "never sleep".
Quiet for3 days

The rail today treats every client as equally important. On any given day, two are. Right-click a client group and choose Pin to Today; it moves into a band above the tree with its chats open. The rest of Clients collapses to a single line of names with status dots, so you still see that Jacki Short has a chat waiting, without her taking six rows.

  • Pins clear at the end of the day or when you unpin, so the band never becomes a second tree.
  • The band is where a saved-session resume lands, so "start on Kittle" is one click.

Half of the height in your screenshot is the word Exited. A row only needs a second line when it has news: running, waiting on you, bell rang. Everything else becomes one line with a dot: green live, orange waiting, hollow exited. Group headers gain a summary, 1 live · 1 waiting, so a collapsed group still reports.

  • Density is a toggle in the rail header (top left of the mock), not a setting buried in preferences.
  • Pairs with idea 3: the folded "everyone else" list is quiet rows by definition.

Hiding and sleeping keep rows; sometimes you want them gone. Tidy on a group proposes a checklist: every exited chat, any two chats with the same name and folder, anything asleep longer than a month. Nothing closes until you press Close selected. Whatever you close lands in Recently closed in the rail footer with its folder, so a chat you regret closing is one click from being back, cwd and all.

  • Recently closed keeps the last 20 and survives restarts.
  • Tidy never touches a live process or anything pinned to Today.

How they fit together. One shelf mechanism serves hide (1), sleep (2) and tidy (5): a row is either in the tree, on the group's shelf, or in Recently closed, and the count in the group header always adds up to all three. Today (3) decides which groups get the full tree at all, and quiet rows (4) decide what a tree row costs. If you wanted only two of the five, take 1 and 4: they remove the most clutter with the least new behaviour.
